# FlōGolf System — Proposal

**Prepared for:** FlōGolf Lounge
**Date:** March 2026

---

## Overview

FlōGolf already has the hard part figured out — a premium experience, strong demand, and multiple revenue streams across bookings, memberships, lessons, leagues, events, and food & drink.

The opportunity isn't about getting more customers through the door. It's about making sure the ones who are already coming back don't slip through the cracks between disconnected tools and manual follow-up.

This proposal lays out how to build a unified internal system for FlōGolf — in phases, with nothing going offline, and each phase delivering value on its own.

---

## What This Solves

Right now, each part of the business likely lives in its own tool or workflow:

- **Bookings** go through one platform
- **Memberships** require manual activation and follow-up after purchase
- **Lessons** need back-and-forth scheduling after checkout
- **Events** run through a separate inquiry and coordination flow
- **Leagues** involve weekly pairings, standings, and communication
- **Gift cards** are sold but redemption and follow-up are disconnected
- **Customer data** is spread across all of the above with no single view

That fragmentation means staff are manually connecting the dots, customers drop off between steps, and there's no single place to see what's actually happening across the business.

The FlōGolf System connects all of that into one operating layer — so every customer, every booking, every membership, and every event lead is tracked, followed up on, and reported in one place.

---

## Phased Approach

Nothing gets replaced on day one. Each phase layers on top of the current operation. Existing tools keep running until the new system proves it handles that area better. Every phase is approved before it starts.

**My recommendation:** start with the area that has the most immediate revenue impact and the least disruption. Based on what I've seen, that's likely **Phase 1 or Phase 2** — but I'd want to confirm that with a quick look at how things are actually running behind the scenes.

---

## Phase 1 — Bookings + Customer Profiles
> *Get one view of every customer and tighten the booking flow.*

**Timeline:** 2–3 weeks
**Investment:** $2,500 – $4,000

**What gets built:**
- Unified customer database — every guest gets one profile that tracks bookings, memberships, lessons, leagues, events, and spend history
- Booking workflow automation — confirmations, reminders, arrival instructions, no-show follow-up
- Repeat booking prompts — nudge past guests to rebook, especially for off-peak slots
- Owner dashboard — today's bookings, revenue snapshot, and a clear view of what needs attention

**What this changes:**
- No more scattered customer info across platforms
- Fewer no-shows
- More repeat bookings without staff chasing people down
- One place to see every customer's full history with FlōGolf

---

## Phase 2 — Memberships + Lessons
> *Close the gap between purchase and activation. Stop losing members and lesson buyers to manual handoffs.*

**Timeline:** 3–4 weeks
**Investment:** $3,500 – $5,500

**What gets built:**
- Membership instant activation — purchase triggers access instructions, welcome email, and perk breakdown automatically
- Membership lifecycle management — renewal reminders, perk usage tracking, win-back outreach for lapsed members
- Lesson intake automation — purchase triggers scheduling flow instead of email back-and-forth
- Lesson package tracking — track sessions used, follow up when a package is running low, prompt re-purchase
- Retention campaigns — automated follow-up after lessons to keep students engaged

**What this changes:**
- Members don't have to contact support after purchasing to get activated
- Lesson buyers don't wait for manual scheduling
- Staff spend less time on follow-up that the system handles
- Renewals and retention happen consistently instead of when someone remembers

---

## Phase 3 — Events + Lead Pipeline
> *Turn event inquiries into booked revenue with automated follow-up and a clear deal pipeline.*

**Timeline:** 2–3 weeks
**Investment:** $3,000 – $5,000

**What gets built:**
- Event pipeline — every inquiry tracked through stages: Inquiry → Quote → Deposit → Booked
- Automated lead follow-up — immediate response to new inquiries, timed follow-up sequences until booked or closed
- Lead qualification — capture group size, budget, event type, and preferred dates upfront
- Deposit and reminder workflows — automate payment reminders and pre-event coordination
- Event reporting — see pipeline value, close rates, and where leads are dropping off

**What this changes:**
- Event leads don't go cold because follow-up is automatic
- Every inquiry is tracked and visible, not buried in email
- Clear view of how much revenue is in the pipeline at any point
- Staff spend time on closing deals, not chasing them

## Phase 4 — Leagues + Communications
> *Streamline league operations and build a communication layer across the entire business.*

**Timeline:** 3–4 weeks
**Investment:** $3,000 – $5,000

**What gets built:**
- League registration and management — confirmations, weekly reminders, pairings, standings, and recap updates
- Season re-enrollment — automated campaigns to bring players back next season
- Unified communications — one system for booking confirmations, membership updates, event follow-ups, league reminders, and promotional outreach
- Gift card lifecycle — track purchases, prompt redemption, convert recipients into repeat guests
- Review and referral prompts — post-visit review requests and referral campaigns

**What this changes:**
- League coordination stops being a manual weekly task
- Communication goes out consistently across every part of the business
- Gift cards actually drive repeat visits instead of sitting unused
- Reviews and referrals happen automatically after good experiences

## Phase 5 — Reporting + Multi-Location
> *Full operational visibility and a foundation built to scale.*

**Timeline:** 3–4 weeks
**Investment:** $3,500 – $5,500

**What gets built:**
- Revenue reporting — track where revenue actually comes from across bookings, memberships, lessons, events, and leagues
- Conversion tracking — measure how customers move from first visit to membership, lessons, leagues, and events
- Drop-off identification — see exactly where customers fall out of the journey
- Multi-location architecture — location-specific schedules, bays, and staff views with shared customer data and centralized reporting
- Cross-location support — shared memberships, promotions, and performance comparison between locations

**What this changes:**
- Clear picture of what's driving revenue and what's underperforming
- Data-driven decisions instead of gut feel
- Opening a second location doesn't mean doubling the operational complexity
- One system scales across locations instead of multiplying disconnected tools

Before building anything in Phase 1, I'll do a quick review of the current tools and workflows to confirm where the biggest gaps are. That review is included — it's not a separate charge. It makes sure whatever gets built first is pointed at the right problem.

From there, each phase follows the same pattern:

1. **Review** — Confirm how that area works today and where the leaks are
2. **Build** — Develop the system layer for that area
3. **Deploy** — Launch alongside existing tools, no disruption
4. **Validate** — Confirm it's working before moving to the next phase

Existing tools stay running until the new system proves it handles the workflow better.
